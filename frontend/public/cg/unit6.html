01. Light Sources MEDIUM
How does light reach our eyes from an object?
Definition

A Light Source is any object that emits light energy (photons) which travels through a medium and strikes a surface, part of which is then reflected toward the viewer's eye, enabling the surface to be perceived/rendered as visible and coloured.

🇮🇳 Hinglish Explanation
Light source wo cheez hai jo light emit karti hai — jaise Sun ya bulb. Yeh light kisi object pe padti hai, kuch part absorb hota hai aur kuch reflect hokar humari aankhon tak pahunchta hai. Isi reflected light ki wajah se hum object ka color aur shape dekh paate hain.

Types of Light Sources

TypeDescriptionExample
Point Light SourceLight originates from a single point, rays spread radiallyBulb, candle
Distributed/Area Light SourceLight comes from an extended area/surfaceTube light, sky on cloudy day
Ambient LightLight scattered uniformly from all directions in environment (no specific source/direction)General room lighting
Directional/Parallel LightLight rays travel parallel (source assumed at infinity)Sunlight
Why It Matters

In computer graphics, we must simulate how a light source interacts with object surfaces to compute pixel intensity/colour — this simulation is called an Illumination Model.

02. Basic Illumination Models HIGH

Definition

An Illumination Model (or Lighting Model) is a mathematical formula used to calculate the intensity of light reflected at a given point on an object's surface, based on light source properties, surface material properties, and the relative geometry (angles) between the light, surface, and viewer.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
Illumination model ek formula hai jisse hum calculate karte hain ki kisi surface ke point pe kitni light intensity reflect hogi. Yeh light source ki properties, surface material, aur angle pe depend karta hai.

Components of Basic Illumination Model

ComponentDescription
Ambient ReflectionConstant background illumination present everywhere, regardless of light source direction
Diffuse ReflectionLight scattered equally in all directions from a rough/matte surface; depends on angle of incidence (Lambert's Cosine Law)
Specular ReflectionLight reflected in a concentrated direction producing shiny highlights on smooth/glossy surfaces

Ambient Light Model

I = Ia · Ka
where Ia = intensity of ambient light, Ka = ambient-reflection coefficient of the surface (0≤Ka≤1)

Diffuse Reflection — Lambert's Cosine Law

Lambert's Law states that the intensity of light reflected by a perfectly diffuse (matte) surface is directly proportional to the cosine of the angle θ between the light direction and the surface normal.

Idiff = Ip · Kd · cosθ = Ip · Kd · (N · L)
where Ip = point light source intensity, Kd = diffuse-reflection coefficient, N = unit surface normal, L = unit vector toward light source

Fig 1: Angle θ between Normal N and Light Vector L

Diffuse Reflection with Ambient + Attenuation

I = Ia·Ka + Ip·Kd·(N·L)

Specular Reflection — Phong Model

The Phong Specular Reflection Model approximates shiny highlights by considering the angle φ between the viewer direction V and the ideal reflection vector R, raised to a power n (specular-reflection/shininess exponent).

Ispec = Ip · Ks · cosⁿφ = Ip · Ks · (R · V)ⁿ
SymbolMeaning
KsSpecular-reflection coefficient (material property)
nSpecular-reflection exponent — higher n = shinier, smaller highlight
RDirection of ideal (mirror) reflection of light
VDirection toward viewer/camera

Combined (Total) Illumination Equation

I = Ia·Ka  +  Ip·Kd·(N·L)  +  Ip·Ks·(R·V)ⁿ
[ Ambient  +  Diffuse  +  Specular ]
⚠ Important

This is the classical Phong Illumination Model — one of the most frequently asked 10-mark questions in RTU CGM papers. Remember all three components and their physical meaning.

Example

If Ia=0.4, Ka=0.5, Ip=0.8, Kd=0.6, θ=60° (cos60°=0.5):
I (ambient+diffuse) = 0.4×0.5 + 0.8×0.6×0.5 = 0.2 + 0.24 = 0.44

03. Halftone Patterns & Dithering Techniques HIGH

Definition

Halftoning is a technique used to simulate a continuous range of shades/intensity levels on a device (printer/display) capable of producing only a limited number of intensity levels (often just black and white), by varying the size and/or density of printed dots in a small area.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
Halftoning ka use hota hai jab device sirf limited colors/shades print kar sakta hai (jaise newspaper printing sirf black/white karta hai), lekin humein grey shades dikhani hain. Solution: chhote-chhote dots ka pattern banao — dots jitne bade/dense honge, area utna dark dikhega, jitne chhote/sparse honge utna light dikhega.

Halftone Approximation (for Bilevel Display)

Group several pixels together into a halftone cell/pattern (e.g. 2×2, 3×3) and turn ON a number of pixels proportional to the desired intensity level, creating the illusion of grey using only black and white dots.

Grid SizePossible Intensity Levels
2×2 cell5 levels (0 to 4 dots ON)
3×3 cell10 levels (0 to 9 dots ON)
n×n celln²+1 levels

Fig 2: Halftone Cell Approximation of Intensity Levels (3×3 grid)

Dithering

Definition

Dithering is the technique of approximating an image with a limited colour/intensity palette by adding controlled "noise" (random or patterned variation) to pixel values before quantization, so the eye visually averages nearby dots to perceive intermediate shades not actually present in the palette.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
Dithering halftoning ka hi ek refined version hai jisme random/pattern noise add karke colors ko arrange karte hain taki overall image zyada smooth aur natural dikhe, bina banding/blocky patterns ke.
TypeDescription
Ordered DitheringUses a fixed threshold matrix (dither matrix) applied repeatedly across the image
Random DitheringAdds random noise values to pixel intensity before thresholding — avoids regular patterns
Error Diffusion (Floyd-Steinberg)Quantization error of one pixel is propagated/distributed to neighbouring unprocessed pixels
Halftoning vs Dithering — Quick Difference
HalftoningDithering
Varies dot SIZE/density in a cellAdds noise/pattern before quantizing pixel value
Mainly used in printingMainly used in digital displays/images

04. Properties of Light MEDIUM

Definition

Light is electromagnetic radiation perceivable by the human eye, characterized by a continuous spectrum of wavelengths roughly between 400 nm (violet) and 700 nm (red); the perceived colour of light is determined by its dominant wavelength composition.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
Light electromagnetic energy hai jiski wavelength humari aankh dekh sakti hai (~400-700 nm). Different wavelengths different colors dikhati hain — jaise short wavelength = violet/blue, long wavelength = red.

Key Properties / Attributes of Colour Perception

PropertyDescription
HueThe dominant wavelength — what we normally call "colour" (red, green, blue, etc.)
SaturationPurity of the colour — how much it is diluted with white light (high saturation = vivid colour)
Lightness/BrightnessThe perceived intensity of the light (luminance) — how light or dark the colour appears
ChromaticityCombination of hue and saturation (excludes brightness)
Example

Sky blue vs Navy blue have the same hue but different saturation/lightness — sky blue is lighter/less saturated, navy blue is darker/more saturated.

05. Standard Primaries & Chromaticity Diagram HIGH

Definition

The CIE Standard Primaries (Red, Green, Blue — also called XYZ primaries) are a set of three reference colours defined by the CIE (Commission Internationale de l'Eclairage) in 1931, chosen such that any visible colour can be matched by some positive combination/mixture of these three primaries.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
Standard primaries ek fixed set of 3 reference colors hain (CIE ke according) jinko mix karke koi bhi visible color banaya ja sakta hai. Isi base pe chromaticity diagram banaya gaya hai jisse colors ko plot/compare kiya ja sakta hai.

CIE Chromaticity Diagram

Definition

The Chromaticity Diagram is a 2D graph (x,y) that represents all visible colours independent of their intensity/brightness, where x and y are normalized chromaticity values derived from the tristimulus values X, Y, Z (x = X/(X+Y+Z), y = Y/(X+Y+Z)).

Fig 3: CIE Chromaticity Diagram — Horseshoe boundary = pure spectral colours; triangle = displayable gamut for RGB monitors

Key Properties of the Diagram

  • Pure spectral colours (fully saturated) lie on the curved boundary
  • White/grey (achromatic) point lies near the centre
  • A straight line joining any two points represents all colours obtainable by mixing those two colours
  • The triangle formed by any 3 primaries (e.g. RGB monitor phosphors) represents the colour gamut — the set of all colours that device can reproduce
  • Complementary colours lie on a straight line through the white point, on opposite sides
⚠ Important

No 3 real primaries can cover the entire visible colour region (the horseshoe) — this is why every display device (monitor/printer) has a limited colour gamut and cannot reproduce all real-world colours.

06. Intuitive Colour Concepts MEDIUM

Definition

Intuitive Colour Concepts refer to the artist's natural way of describing colour using three perceptually meaningful terms: Hue (the colour itself), Shade (hue mixed with black, also called tint when mixed with white), and Tone (hue mixed with grey) — rather than numeric RGB-style values.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
Artists numbers mein nahi sochte (jaise RGB=255,0,0), woh intuitively sochte hain — "yeh pure red hai", "isme thoda black mix karo (shade)", "isme thoda white mix karo (tint)", "isme thoda grey mix karo (tone)". Yeh natural human way hai colors describe karne ki.
TermMeaning
Pure HueFully saturated spectral colour with no black/white/grey added
TintHue + White added (lighter version)
ShadeHue + Black added (darker version)
ToneHue + Grey added (both black and white)

07. RGB Colour Model HIGH

Definition

The RGB Model represents colours as an additive combination of three primary colours — Red, Green, Blue — represented as a unit cube in 3D space, where each axis ranges from 0 to 1 (or 0–255), and every point inside the cube represents a unique colour.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
RGB model mein colors ko Red, Green, Blue light ko add (mix) karke banaya jaata hai. Yeh monitors, TVs aur digital screens ka standard model hai kyunki light emit hoti hai (additive mixing).

Fig 4: RGB Colour Cube

CombinationResulting Colour
R+GYellow
G+BCyan
R+BMagenta
R+G+BWhite
No ComponentBlack
Example

Pure Yellow = R(1,0,0) + G(0,1,0) = (1,1,0). Orange ≈ (1, 0.5, 0)

08. YIQ Colour Model MEDIUM

Definition

The YIQ Model is the colour model adopted for commercial colour TV broadcasting (NTSC standard), where Y represents luminance (brightness — the only signal a black-and-white TV needs), and I, Q represent chrominance (colour information).

🇮🇳 Hinglish
YIQ TV broadcasting ke liye banaya gaya tha. Y mein brightness info hoti hai — purane black & white TV sirf Y use karte the. I aur Q mein color info hoti hai jo color TVs use karte hain. Isse backward compatibility milti thi.
Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B
I = 0.596R − 0.274G − 0.322B
Q = 0.211R − 0.523G + 0.312B
Why Y this way?

The weights in Y reflect human eye sensitivity — eyes are most sensitive to Green, then Red, least to Blue. This is why Y = 0.299R+0.587G+0.114B (Green has highest weight).

09. CMY Colour Model HIGH

Definition

The CMY Model (Cyan, Magenta, Yellow) is a subtractive colour model used in colour printing, where each primary represents the amount of that ink applied to a (white) surface, and colour is produced by subtracting (absorbing) wavelengths from white light rather than adding them.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
CMY printing ke liye use hota hai. RGB mein light add hoti hai (additive) jabki CMY mein ink white surface se light ko absorb (subtract) karti hai. Jaise yellow ink blue light ko absorb karti hai, baaki reflect karti hai jo yellow dikhta hai.
C = 1 − R
M = 1 − G
Y = 1 − B
FeatureRGB (Additive)CMY (Subtractive)
Used inMonitors, TVs, screensPrinters, printing press
Mixing TypeAdd lightSubtract/absorb light from white
Black formed byAbsence of all (0,0,0)Sum of all inks (C+M+Y=1)
Starting BackgroundBlack screenWhite paper
⚠ Important — CMYK

In real printing, pure C+M+Y rarely gives true black (gives muddy brown) — so a 4th ink, Black (K), is added → CMYK model, used by all commercial colour printers to save ink cost and get truer blacks.

Example

RGB = (0.2, 0.5, 0.8) → CMY = (1−0.2, 1−0.5, 1−0.8) = (0.8, 0.5, 0.2)

10. HSV Colour Model HIGH

Definition

The HSV Model (Hue, Saturation, Value) represents colour using a hexagonal-cone (or cylinder) coordinate system that closely matches how artists/humans intuitively perceive colour: Hue (which colour, 0°–360°), Saturation (purity/vividness, 0–1), and Value (brightness, 0–1).

🇮🇳 Hinglish
HSV model human-friendly hai — Hue batata hai konsa color hai (red/green/blue etc), Saturation batata hai color kitna pure/vivid hai (white se kitna mix hai), Value batata hai color kitna bright/dark hai. Yeh color-picker tools mein commonly use hota hai.

Fig 5: HSV Colour Model (Hexcone/Cone shape)

AxisRangeMeaning
Hue (H)0°–360°Angular position — which colour (0°=Red, 120°=Green, 240°=Blue)
Saturation (S)0–1Distance from centre axis — purity of colour (0=grey, 1=pure)
Value (V)0–1Height along the cone — brightness (0=black, 1=brightest)
Why HSV is Used

Unlike RGB cube where changing "brightness" requires changing all 3 values together, HSV separates intensity (Value) from colour (Hue, Saturation) — making it intuitive for colour-picker UIs and image-editing tools (e.g., Photoshop colour wheel).

11. HLS Colour Model MEDIUM

Definition

The HLS Model (Hue, Lightness, Saturation), developed by Tektronix, represents colour space as a double cone (bi-cone) with pure black at the bottom apex, pure white at the top apex, and fully saturated hues around the middle equator — giving symmetric control over lightening and darkening a colour.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
HLS HSV jaisa hi hai but shape double-cone (do cones jude hue) hai. Top pe white, bottom pe black, beech mein full saturation. Lightness 0 se 1 tak jaata hai — 0.5 pe pure colours milte hain, isse zyada/kam karne se white/black mix hota hai.

Fig 6: HLS Colour Model (Double-cone shape)

Top of shapeBrightest colour (not necessarily white)Always pure white

12. Colour Selection LOW

Definition

Colour Selection refers to the process and guidelines used to choose appropriate, perceptually effective, and harmonious colour combinations for a graphical application/interface, considering factors such as contrast, harmony, purpose, and viewer perception.

🇮🇳 Hinglish
Colour selection ka matlab hai application ya design ke liye sahi colors choose karna — jisse readability, contrast aur visual appeal sab ache rahe. Random colors use karne se design bigad sakta hai.

Guidelines for Effective Colour Selection

  • Use a limited number of colours (avoid overload) — typically 5-7 max in a design
  • Use warm colours (red/orange) to highlight/draw attention, cool colours (blue/green) for backgrounds
  • Ensure adequate contrast between foreground/background for readability
  • Maintain consistency — same colour for same meaning throughout the application
  • Consider colour-blind users — avoid relying purely on hue differences (use brightness/pattern too)
  • Use complementary/harmonious colour schemes from chromaticity relationships

Q1 · 10 MARKS
10 MARKS
Explain the basic illumination model. Derive the combined equation for ambient, diffuse, and specular reflection (Phong model).

Ambient Reflection: Constant background light present everywhere: I = Ia·Ka

Diffuse Reflection (Lambert's Law): Light scattered equally in all directions from a matte surface, proportional to cosθ between surface normal N and light vector L:

Idiff = Ip·Kd·(N·L)

Specular Reflection (Phong Model): Concentrated reflection causing shiny highlight, depends on angle between viewer V and ideal reflection R, raised to power n:

Ispec = Ip·Ks·(R·V)ⁿ

Combined Illumination Equation:

I = Ia·Ka + Ip·Kd·(N·L) + Ip·Ks·(R·V)ⁿ

Each term contributes independently to the final colour/brightness at a surface point, and they are summed to give the total perceived intensity used in rendering.

Q2 · 10 MARKS
10 MARKS
What is the CIE Chromaticity Diagram? Explain its construction and properties with a diagram.

The CIE Chromaticity Diagram is a 2D plot of normalized chromaticity coordinates x = X/(X+Y+Z), y = Y/(X+Y+Z), derived from CIE XYZ tristimulus values, representing all visible colours independent of brightness.

Properties:

  • Pure spectral (fully saturated) colours lie along the curved (horseshoe) boundary
  • White/achromatic point lies near the centre of the diagram
  • A line joining two points represents all colours obtainable by mixing them
  • A triangle formed by 3 chosen primaries represents the device's colour gamut
  • No 3 real primaries can reproduce the entire visible region — hence limited gamut for any device

This diagram is used to compare colour gamuts of different display/printing devices and to determine complementary colours (lying on opposite sides of the white point on a straight line through it).

Compare RGB, CMY, HSV and HLS colour models in detail with diagrams.
ModelComponentsTypeUsed For

RGB is represented as a cube — additive mixing where R+G+B=White, used wherever light is emitted (screens). CMY is the complementary subtractive model where ink absorbs colour from white light (C=1−R, M=1−G, Y=1−B), used in printing (often extended to CMYK for true black). HSV represents colour as a single cone with Hue as angle, Saturation as radius, Value as height — separates colour from brightness intuitively. HLS is similar but uses a double-cone shape with symmetric Lightness axis (black at bottom, white at top, pure hues at the middle equator) giving symmetric control for lightening/darkening.

Q4 · 10 MARKS
10 MARKS
What is halftoning? Explain halftone approximation and dithering techniques with examples.

Halftoning simulates continuous shades on a device capable of producing only limited intensity levels, by varying the size/density of printed dots in a cell.

Halftone Approximation: Pixels are grouped into n×n cells; turning ON a number of dots proportional to the desired grey level creates the illusion of intermediate shades. An n×n cell gives n²+1 distinguishable intensity levels (e.g., 3×3 → 10 levels).

Dithering: Approximates an image using a limited palette by adding controlled noise before quantization so neighbouring dots visually average to perceived intermediate colours not actually in the palette.

Types of Dithering: Ordered dithering (fixed threshold matrix), Random dithering (random noise added), and Error diffusion/Floyd-Steinberg (propagates quantization error to neighbouring pixels) — the latter gives the smoothest results and is widely used in image processing software.

Q6 · 5 MARKS
5 MARKS
Explain Lambert's Cosine Law for diffuse reflection.

Lambert's Cosine Law states that the intensity of light diffusely reflected from a matte surface is proportional to the cosine of the angle θ between the surface normal N and the direction L toward the light source:

Idiff = Ip · Kd · cosθ = Ip · Kd · (N·L)

When the light is directly perpendicular to the surface (θ=0°), reflection is maximum (cos0°=1). As θ increases toward 90°, reflected intensity decreases to zero, since cosθ→0 — surfaces facing away from light appear dark.

Q7 · 5 MARKS
5 MARKS
What is the YIQ colour model? Why was it developed?

YIQ is the colour model used for NTSC colour TV broadcasting. Y represents luminance (brightness, needed by black-and-white TVs), I and Q carry chrominance (colour) information, allowing backward compatibility with older monochrome televisions.

Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B
I = 0.596R − 0.274G − 0.322B
Q = 0.211R − 0.523G + 0.312B

The Y weights match the human eye's higher sensitivity to green light, then red, then blue.

Q8 · 5 MARKS — Short Note
5 MARKS
Write a short note on the HSV colour model.

HSV represents colour using Hue (angular position 0°-360° denoting which colour), Saturation (radial distance, 0-1, denoting purity/vividness of colour), and Value (height, 0-1, denoting brightness). It is shaped as a single cone/hexcone, with black at the apex (V=0) and the brightest, most saturated colours around the top rim. HSV is widely used in colour-picker tools because it separates brightness from hue/saturation, matching how humans intuitively think about colour.

Q10 · 5 MARKS
5 MARKS
Explain different types of light sources used in illumination models.

Point Light Source: Originates from a single point, rays diverge radially (e.g. bulb).

Distributed/Area Light: Light emitted from an extended surface/area (e.g. tube light, overcast sky).

Ambient Light: Uniformly scattered background light from all directions with no identifiable source/direction; contributes a constant base illumination to every surface.

Directional/Parallel Light: Light rays travel parallel to each other, as if the source were at infinite distance (e.g. sunlight).
